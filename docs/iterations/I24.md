## Surprises

1. **The linked-fixture renaming test could not pass as preregistered.** Its map sent `Root` to `ZqOld` and `RootNew` to `ZqNew`, which reverses the two getters' order, and rule P4 names a PAR-weak law and orders its claim by the getters' names. The prereg instrument itself (`tests_m3/_i24x.py`) derives `L_par_weak_HdrBytes_ZqNew_HdrBytes_ZqOld_v2` for the renamed copy, where the renamed original is `..._ZqOld_HdrBytes_ZqNew_v2`. Amended (`spec/M3/amendments.jsonl`, kind `instrument_inconsistency`): `RootNew` now maps to `ZqOldNew`, an order-preserving renaming under which the instrument's two derivations agree law for law. No assertion changed.
2. **The honesty test forbids quoting the Go document's layout key in `holon/diagram/linked.py`.** The key is spelled like a name a target document uses, so the generic layer may not quote it. The accessor moved to the Go frontend's own module, `holon/frontends/go/document.py` (`encoder_layout`), which is where the document format belongs anyway.
3. **The prereg left the ten facts fixtures without a ledger bundle**, while the contract requires their records to validate under v2 (`ledger_match` "exact"; a test milestone without a bundle writes "none", which v2 never accepts). `tests_m3/fixtures/ledger.json` is new: one bundle per fixture, its entries the fixture's own declared assumptions (0.1) or abstractions (0.2), written by a generator from the fixtures. No preregistered file changed.
4. **A linked codec document's ledger bundle is generated, not committed.** `holon.records` resolves it through `holon.gen.linked.declared_bundle`: every assumption the two extracted documents declare, introduced by the definitions generated from that document, plus the layer's own `A-codec-domain` (encoder domains are not modelled, rule X5). The document is itself generated, so a committed bundle would have to be regenerated with it; G9 recomputes the same bundle. A Morph cross-component record lists 12 ledger assumptions.
5. **The generator closures of holon-ir/0.2 and 0.3 contain almost all of `holon/`.** I24's changes to `ir.py`, `refs.py`, `mlaws.py`, `mproofs.py`, `records.py` and the pipelines change `generator_sha256` of every M1 and M2 record (and nothing else: the emitted Bend, statuses, witnesses and cones are unchanged). M1 and M2 were re-run and their records committed; I30, built on the same base, will do the same, so the merge needs one more re-run of M1 and M2. M0's closure (`facts.py`, `gen/laws.py`, `gen/model.py`, `gen/proofs.py`) is untouched and M0 is byte-identical.
6. **Two schemas grew additively**: `spec/schemas/law_ir_0_3.schema.json` (the codec and expr terms) and `spec/schemas/evidence.schema.json` (`facts_version` may be `holon-linked/0.1`, the component models of an M3 evidence set).
7. **A test milestone has no committed evidence set.** Its PAR records take their evidence fields from the set its derivation built, rebuilt in memory (with the sorts assignment `holon sorts` gives the same documents) and used only when its `evidence_sha256` equals `DERIVATION.json`'s; otherwise they keep null levels. Without this the linked fixture's PAR-weak law could not be an intent question in its record.
8. **The leak report (test_partial_gate_m3_passes printing tests_m2.test_mutation_adequacy_i60 output) did not reproduce.** In this worktree the test passed (595 s together with the fixture outcomes) and its log holds only unittest's own lines. The test prints only its gate subprocess's captured stdout and stderr (`tests/_util.run_holon` uses `capture_output`), no holon module runs unittest or imports a test module, and the I60 module captures every subprocess it starts. So the leaked text cannot come from the gate subprocess; the likeliest source is another unittest process writing to the same terminal or log. One hazard is real, though not the cause: `tests/_util.copy_repo` ignores `.git` but not `.claude`, so a copy made from the main checkout copies every agent worktree under `.claude/worktrees/` (slow, and it puts other agents' trees inside the copy). Nothing in I24's code is involved, so nothing was changed.
9. **Three M2-era checks contradict I24's contract outputs** and were amended in `spec/M2/amendments.jsonl` (`foundation_conflict`; the prereg audit lists them under I6, I15 and I10):
   - `test_target_agnostic.test_bindings_unchanged` pins the `spec/bindings` tree, and `spec/bindings/M3.json` is a deliverable. It is skipped like M2.json; M0's and M1's bindings stay pinned.
   - `test_sort_sites.TestGenericCode.test_the_rule_code_names_no_target_and_no_reason_text` forbids any Morph name as a string literal in `holon/rules/*.py`. PAR's contract vocabulary uses four strings that happen to be Morph names: the cycle_rank key "kappa", the totality law-id tag "tot", the E4 atom key "stored" and the record field "version". Those four are exempt in `par.py` and `__init__.py` only.
   - `test_regress_a3` counted the record transitions of M0 and M1 by subtracting M2's; it now subtracts M3's too and checks that they are exactly the committed M3 records.
10. **The machine was heavily loaded by parallel builds**, at load averages up to 20 on 4 cores and with the disk at 95%. The first frozen-suite run failed only `test_40_gate`: G7 Lean took 189 s on one law against the 120 s budget, and a G1 copy hit "No space left on device". `holon gate --milestone M0` then passed on its own (173 s), as recorded above.
