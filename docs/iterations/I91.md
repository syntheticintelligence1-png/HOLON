# I91 (= I38c): the compression oracle, HypZ and HypZ_len, tested on the real codec at K = 126,975. 6 of 6 predictions held (5 counted, 1 recorded)

I91 gives the compression half of I38 its two hypotheses (whitepaper §8.5; distinct from D2's Hyp-Z0-kzg) and tests them
on the real codec, without a proof. A model of the batch codec takes the compressor Enc and the decompressor Dec as
uninterpreted parameters, under:
- **HypZ:** forall x non-empty, k <= K. Dec(Enc x ++ 0^k) = x. The derivation node concatenates a batch's blob bodies
  and decompresses them once, so a compressed payload arrives followed by the zero bytes of its last blob.
- **HypZ_len:** |Enc x| is at most zstd's compress bound of |x| (ZSTD_COMPRESSBOUND).

`holon oracle compression --milestone M3` (new, `holon/oracles/compression.py`) reads the sidecar
`spec/M3/lib/compression.json` and generates a Go test from the names it holds. The test runs in a scratch copy of the
pinned checkout's go.work modules:
- Enc is the submitter's compressor and Dec the derivation's decompressor; both link the real zstd (the cgo binding
  `github.com/morph-l2/morph-da-codec/bindings/codec`, v0.0.0-20260727095527-681727561e38).
- Between them runs the real blob pipeline: the submitter's sidecar builder, the derivation's per-blob reader and the
  concatenation of the blob bodies.
- The sample is fixed: zero, ramp and stream (SHA-256 counter) bytes of 11 lengths around the blob capacity, 9 explicit
  paddings up to K and 2 past it, a 1,024-length expansion window and the empty payload.

The result is `results/M3/compression_oracle.json`. I91 changes no law, record, rule catalog, frontend, Go document or
model, so nothing is re-made.

**What the real code does:**
- **K = 126,975 = C - 1**, C = 126,976 the blob capacity (4096 * 31). The submitter splits a compressed payload over
  the fewest blobs, so the zero run after it is at most C - 1, whatever maxBlobCount is. Every one of the 33 sample
  points shows it: padded length = blobs * C, the bodies are the payload and then zeros only, paddings 122,874 to
  126,970.
- **HypZ: checked** (a test on the sample, not a proof): 33 points, 330 decodes (the pipeline and 9 paddings each), no
  failure.
- **HypZ_len: checked:** at every point |Enc x| <= CB(|x|); the largest |Enc x| - |x| is 11 (2C stream bytes, two raw
  blocks).
- **Expansion at the smallest blob limit** (maxBlobCount 1, L = 126,976): incompressible stream bytes of length L
  encode to 126,984 bytes, an overhead of 8. Over [L - 1023, L] the lengths that exceed L are exactly
  126,969..126,976. So the non-expansion the estimators' fast path assumes (O5) fails on the real codec, from 126,969
  bytes on. That is the witness I93's O5 law and replay need.
- **Zero runs past K decode too** (k = C and 2C at every point). K bounds what the honest pipeline appends, not what
  Dec accepts.
- **The empty payload does not decode.** Enc maps it to nil, Dec of the empty string fails with `empty input`, and the
  one all-zero blob the split builds for it fails with `morph da zstd decode: invalid frame` (Surprise 3,
  Question 4).

**Counted:** I91-P2 to P6. I91-P1 is recorded: its constants were read before the prereg.

## Surprises

1. **O6 and D3's height law were not the cheap half.** The brief expected them to land soonest; at the prereg neither
   had a route under I67's no-catalog-change pattern (key `thresholds.split`):
   - **D3's height round trip** is already in the facts: `go.codecs` holds BuildBlockContext's 60-byte encoder, whose
     Number cells are exact on both BaseFee paths, and the height decoder's be_uint read of bytes 0-7. But rule PAR's
     round-trip producer P1 reads E1 inverse atoms, and `holon.diagram.evidence` computes them per version only.
     P1's other branch needs a holon-facts/0.1 layout whose model (`holon.gen.model`) requires a 1-byte version field.
     Its linked branch (X8) reads only `holon.diagram.linked`'s components, which need a Solidity loader and a Go
     tagged predicate. No M3 round-trip law is unversioned.
   - **O6** compares the submitter's legacy Go DataHash (keccak of each block context's first 58 bytes and the L1
     hashes) with Rollup.sol's `_commitBatch` (keccak of lastBlockNumber, numL1Messages and the L1 hashes). That is
     two languages and two preimages. P9 reads one machine document whose E4 atom is one call, stored by one action
     and recomputed by another, and P3 compares two paths of one function.
   - **O5's PAR-branch law** fits P3. But with Enc uninterpreted and only an upper length bound, no counter-law can
     show |Enc w| > limit at a concrete w.

   So I91 lands the hypotheses, and I93 inherits a route decision per law (Question 1).
2. **The real frame format, as observed:** a 1-byte payload encodes to 6 bytes, and 126,976 incompressible bytes to
   126,984. That is consistent with zstd's magic-less format with the content size and no checksum: 1 + 4 header bytes
   and one 3-byte raw-block header. At 2C there are two raw blocks (overhead 11). In the window, the lengths that
   exceed L are exactly L - 7..L, as a constant 8-byte overhead gives. So the smallest incompressible payload the
   one-blob estimator wrongly accepts is 126,969 bytes.
3. **The empty payload's blob does not decode.** I91-P5 predicted it, but its consequence is outside I91's scope. On
   the legacy path, a batch whose payload is empty (blocks without L2 transactions) is compressed to nil and posted as
   one all-zero blob. The derivation's `ParseBatch` would then fail at `DecompressBatchBytes` with `invalid frame`. I
   did not check whether the submitter can seal such a batch (Question 4).
4. **maxBlobCount does not enter K.** The brief proposed deriving K from the blob size and maxBlobCount. On the pinned
   code `MakeBlobTxSidecar` uses ceil(|c| / C) blobs whatever maxBlobs is (maxBlobs only refuses a payload that is too
   large), so the zero run is under one blob. Rollup.sol's V2 blob hash accepts any number of blobs from one, though:
   a batch posted with extra all-zero blobs would hand Dec more zeros. Dec accepted 2C past the payload at every point.
5. **I90's R2 counts I91's commits on this stacked branch.** `tests_m3/test_go_counts.py` judges I90's own commits as
   I90-prereg..HEAD until a merge brings I90 into the lead. On this branch that line includes I91's outcome commit,
   whose `results/M3/compression_oracle.json` is not a record, so `test_r2_nothing_else_changes` fails. This is I90's
   Surprise 5 again (an own-commit walk on an unmerged builder branch). It passes once I90 merges, because the walk then
   follows the merge's second parent. CI does not run that module.
6. **Process.** Before C_K, I compiled the instrument's Go file once with `go test -run '^$'` (no test ran) to catch a
   syntax error before pinning it. I also re-extracted the M3 Go document with its 0.2 config into the scratch
   directory (byte-identical, about 18 s) while sizing I93's frontend work. Both are disclosed in the key. No codec
   call was made before C_K.

## Questions for the human

1. **The split, and I93's routes.** I91 holds the two hypotheses; O5, O6, D3 and the frontend 0.3 config are I93.
   Which route should each law take there?
   - D3: a catalog version (from the lead) for P1 over unversioned Go codecs, or a linked component.
   - O6: a cross-language recompute route.
   - O5: a lower-side length hypothesis, or zstd's frame format in the model.
2. **The K bound.** I91 takes K = C - 1 = 126,975, what the honest pipeline appends, not maxBlobCount * C - 1. Is that
   the bound you mean? The beyond paddings (C and 2C) show Dec accepts more.
3. **HypZ_len's content.** §8.5's text is not in the repository. I91 states HypZ_len as the upper bound, zstd's
   ZSTD_COMPRESSBOUND. Is that it? Or is HypZ_len a lower or exact length on incompressible input, which O5's in-model
   refutation would need?
4. **The empty payload** (Surprise 3). HypZ excludes it, and the real decoder rejects its all-zero blob. Can the
   submitter seal a legacy batch with an empty payload? If so, should a later key state it as a law over the
   derivation path?
5. **zstd's frame format.** It stays out of the model and is exercised by the oracle run, as the brief decided. Do you
   agree?
6. **O5's "omits contexts" half.** On the pinned code, `CalculateCapWithProposalBlock` calls
   `EstimateCompressedSizeWithNewPayload` only for a batch that is not upgraded. That batch's blob is `TxsPayload`, with
   no contexts. `WillExceedCompressedSizeLimit`, contexts included, serves upgraded batches. The case study's mismatch
   then needs one of two things:
   - a batch whose blocks straddle the upgrade time: estimated without contexts, sealed as `TxsPayloadV2`;
   - or `handleBatchSealing`'s fallback to `TxsPayload`.

   Is that a PAR-branch or a PAR-recompute law? I lean to PAR-recompute (the estimator recomputes the blob content the
   sealing compresses).
7. **D3's decode half.** node/types' `DecodeBlockContext` and the equality helper lie outside the M3 document's
   packages. I93 covers BuildBlockContext and HeightFromBlockContextBytes only, unless you extend the document.
8. **Counting.** P1 is recorded. P2-P6 are counted: no codec call was made before the prereg, and P5's first half rests
   on the binding's source text, which I read. Count them as listed if you judge that enough.
