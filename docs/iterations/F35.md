## Done

### 1. Per-let congruence (`holon/gen/equivalence.py`, `Gen.congruence`, `chain_leaf`)

F34 showed that each motive of a storage-write lemma repeated the whole `sstore` chain with every let inlined. Each
`o_i` names `o_{i-1}` twice (`sstore(o5, k, w_or(w_and(sload(o5, k), ..)..))`), so the inlined text grows as 2^i: 142
to 200 KB per lemma. A first version that kept the lets as proof-level lets with small motives (motive size cut 9x)
still ran out of fuel on all four: the size of the motives was not the cost. Reading the pinned kernel
(`bendtt.lean`: `Term.conv` tries syntactic equality, then weak-head normalizes both sides and compares their parts;
an open let is substituted, so a value shared by name is compared again at every copy) located it: the last
conversion compared the old body (with `gx_*` projections and `b_of_w(W{164, ..})`) with the rewritten new body (with
matched fields and the literal byte list), and every one of the 2^i copies of `k` was normalized again.

The generated proof of a leaf reached through a duplicating chain of lets is now:
- for each let i the driver's rest reads, a helper `t_i(xs, v_i)`: the rest of the driver's chain from let i, whose
  body calls the next helper on the next let's value (or is the leaf); and `u_i(xs, v_i)`, the same over the other
  side's let values;
- a lemma `L_i(xs, v_i) : {u_i(xs, v_i) == t_i(xs, v_i)}` over variables: it rewrites the next let's value (each
  changed call, innermost first), bridges what remains different from the other side's text of that let with a lemma
  proved by computation on that one value (`q_<f>__b..: {old value == new value}`, `{==}`), and ends with the next
  `L`;
- the lemma itself rewrites the first let's value the same way and ends with `L` of the first let.

Every conversion then compares one let's value over variables, and the last step compares terms that are
syntactically equal let by let. When the other side's definition is not a chain of lets over the same variables, the
other side's values are the rewritten driver values. A chain with a let whose type is not evident (an annotation
`{e : T}`, a variable of known type, or a call of a model definition) is inlined up to that let, as before.

Generic: chosen by the shape of the definition, never by a name (tests_m2/test_target_agnostic_exhaustive passes).
The flat text stays a candidate (`congruence-flat`, the last class).

### 3. Deferral to the final check (`holon/gen/eqsearch.py`)

A batch file holds the proven lemmas its candidates use, so near the top of the cone every batch check costs about
the whole cone. At load 6 to 10 on this machine that is more than the 180 s `bend.timeout_s`, which is not raised (F33's
wall). From the first batch timeout on, the remaining candidates are **deferred**: taken unchecked, never cached,
reported `DEFERRED` (not PROVED), and decided only by the final whole-proof check in both kernels. A deferred lemma
the final check refuses (by its location) goes back to the search, as a cached text did before. Deferred lemmas are
cached only after the final check accepts the whole proof in both kernels. On :363, 736 of 1308 lemmas were deferred;
all were accepted by the final check on its first verdict. Tests: with every batch check forced to time out, the
planted chain is PROVED by the final check alone and the planted difference never is.

## Surprises

- **Smaller motives were not enough.** The first version cut the four lemmas to about a ninth of their size and
  BendTT still ran out of fuel on all four. The kernel's cost was the final conversion between the two sides' bodies,
  repeated at every inlined copy of a let; it went away only when each let's value was bridged to the other side's text
  separately (Done 1).
- **The per-let form is slower in bend2 for chains that do not duplicate** (about 4x on `ln_revertBatch_8`), hence
  Done 2.
- **The search could not finish within the 180 s batch timeout**, as in F33: at load 6 to 10 every batch near the top
  of the cone timed out. Deferral (Done 3) made the final check the only verdict for 736 of the :363 lemmas, which is
  what F31's criterion makes it anyway.
- **A latent bug in the family class**: two-index families were never exercised (Done 4).
- **bend2's stack overflow is frequent on these files and not cured by a larger OS stack.** On the 7.9 MB :363
  proof, about 7 of 10 bend2 runs at load 6 to 10 overflowed, at about 120 to 180 s; the same bytes passed in the
  others. `ulimit -s 65536` did not change it (1 of 2 runs overflowed). The rebind logged 3 crashes in all (one per law) and
  retried each (Q34). The crash log is in the rebind's `--report` JSON (`equivalence.final.crashes`), not in the acceptance record, which stores no `final` field (corrected at merge).
- **Committed binding_change lines are re-checked by every milestone's G2, and bind re-checks them too.** On a git
  checkout G2 verifies every line of `laws/STATEMENT_DIFF.jsonl` from its artifacts, so gate-M0, gate-M1 and gate-M2
  each re-run bend2 and BendTT on the three 7.9 MB M3 proofs: about 17 to 18 min per gate on a CI runner, inside the
  jobs' 90 to 120 min limits. `holon bind --milestone M3` does the same before it refuses or writes (44 min here at load
  6 to 10, with crash retries), and a refusal for a missing `--accept-change` comes only after those 44 min.
- **The frozen M0 gate in a copied tree** refused the M3 lines (no git history to check them against). Done 7: off
  git, a gate lists another milestone's binding_change lines as not checked.
- **`bend PROOF.bend -o P.bendtt` and the pinned kernel run directly**: ALL PROOFS CHECK on the whole :363 proof in
  229 s (34 MB `.bendtt`), so the kernel's part of `--verdict` is about 4 minutes.
