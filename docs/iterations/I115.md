## The diagnosis (found on the planted fixture, before the prereg)

I112 read the wall as "the composed route never runs `holon bind`". That is half of it. The planted fixture shows the
mechanism. The fixture is a Go package with an interface carrier `Blob` (two ops, each one loop over the blob's bytes)
and a Go machine `Counter` (whose `Bump` the static go_ops role also makes an op of a component).

- The Go-op model (`holon.gen.goloop`) models an op only in its loop class (one loop body over the carrier's cells).
  Any other op is `Unsupported`: its definitions `<op>` and `ok_<op>` are not generated, and `goloop.decide` leaves
  every law naming it UNKNOWN, as its docstring says.
- `holon.codecrun.decide` then computes the law's kernel cone over that model, for the deps index and the record.
  `holon.bindings.kernel_cone` raises `BindingError` ("M.ok_<op> in the statement resolves to no kernel entry"). The
  exception stops the whole milestone runner, so no law of the run gets a record, the decidable ones included.
- On the fixture, the Blob laws are decided and the run then dies on the Counter law. On the corpus, every one of
  the 93 PAR laws is a Go-op law (domain_coherence, totality, weak), so the runner never got past the first law
  over an op outside the class.
- And no `spec/bindings/<M>.json` is ever made for the composed milestone, so G2 could check none of its decided laws.

On Morph every Go-op is in the loop class, so neither path was ever taken.

## What was built

### (1) The binding (Q88 (2))

**The runner's binding check: `holon run --unbound unknown`** (`holon.codecrun.UNBOUND`, set by `holon.pipeline.main_run`;
the composed driver passes it to the milestone runner under protocol `run_unbound "unknown"`).
- A law is bound by the kernel cone of its statement over the model it is decided on. A statement that names a
  definition its model does not define cannot be bound, so it cannot be decided.
- In `holon.codecrun.decide` (the Go-op, linked, count and facts 0.1 laws), the cone of the statement's IR roots is
  computed first. On `BindingError`, nothing is attempted: the record is UNKNOWN, kernel and checker `not_run`, no
  witness and no law or proof file. Its attempts are `[{kind: binding, error}]`, its bound "not decided: the statement
  cannot be bound to its model: ...", and its deps entry has an empty cone. A `BindingError` on the decided statement
  text drops the decision the same way.
- In `holon.mpipeline`'s own loop (machine-document laws), the check runs where the cone was computed, after the
  decision. A law whose cone raises is UNKNOWN the same way, with its law and proof files removed and the binding
  attempt last (a deviation from the prereg's "before", for this path only: it can only drop a decision).
- Only `BindingError` is caught. Without the option, a `BindingError` stops the runner, as before.

**The composed route's binding: `holon bind --unbound skip` and stage `bind`** (protocol `bind "derived"`).
- After the run step, the driver runs `holon bind --milestone M --root <scratch> --unbound skip`.
  `holon.bindings.bind_unbound_skip` binds every derived law with `bind_law`, exactly as `derive` binds route A's
  milestones, into the scratch root's `spec/bindings/<M>.json`. A law that `bind_law` refuses with `BindingError` is
  left out and listed in `spec/bindings/<M>.unbound.json`. The creation is logged in the scratch `changes.jsonl`.
- `--unbound skip` refuses a milestone that already has a bindings file: an existing binding changes only through the
  statement lifecycle (`main_bind`, unchanged). The repository's `spec/bindings` is never written (the scratch root is
  a copy; P2 checks the repository's is unchanged).
- Then `holon_run.binding_check` admits a PROVED or REFUTED record only when its law is bound with the record's
  `statement_hash` and `artifacts.model`. Any other decided record is rewritten UNKNOWN: verdict, witness and law files
  dropped, `attempts + [{kind: binding_check, error}]`. UNKNOWN records are untouched.
- **No family is routed around it.** G2 binds every law of a milestone (`holon.bindings.derive` over
  `ir.load_laws_any`), so every derived law, DISCH and PAR alike, goes through `holon bind` and the check. No runner is
  exempt, so the prereg needed no argument for a binding-free family.
- The stage detail and the row's `bind` field hold aggregates only (laws, bound, unbound, refused). The bindings name
  law ids, so they go to the vault (`<vault>/<id>/bindings/`), never the committed output.
- **The planted controls:**
  - the fixture's Counter totality law (a statement over an op the model leaves out) is UNKNOWN by the binding (P1);
    it is the one law `holon bind` cannot bind (P2);
  - unit level (P3): a REFUTED record whose law is unbound, or bound to another model, or bound with another statement
    hash, is refused, and a correctly bound PROVED record and an UNKNOWN record are byte-identical.
- Every derived record still carries `roles_source` (I110's driver, unchanged; the aggregates' keys keep it).

## Soundness

- **The binding check only removes decisions.** A law that cannot be bound is UNKNOWN with nothing attempted. A
  decided record the check refuses is rewritten UNKNOWN. Neither ever makes a record PROVED or REFUTED.
- **A law that can be bound is decided as before.** It goes through the same decider over the same model, so its
  record differs only by the new opt-in's attempt kinds where they apply (none for a bound law).
- **Why the check is the right one.** A binding is the kernel cone of the statement over its model. A statement that
  names a definition its model does not define is about something the model does not state, so no verdict on that
  model could be about it. G2 refuses such a law for the same reason.
- **The bindings are `holon bind`'s own** (`bind_law`, unchanged), written into the scratch root only.
  `binding_check` compares each decided record with them, so a record from a different statement or model can never
  be counted as decided.
- **No family is exempt.** Every derived law goes through the bind stage and the check.
- **(2) decides nothing.** It can only stop a run from starting.
- Every REFUTED still needs its replay: the replay and filter stages are unchanged (route A's replay is
  `not_applicable` with a null binding, and any REFUTED is handled under Q56).
