## Surprises

1. **One missing rule sidecar blocks every M3 law.**
   - `holon derive --milestone M3` refuses as a whole without `spec/M3/lib/disch.json`, and with one it still refuses
     unless `holon disch`'s outputs are fresh.
   - The PAR part's input, the codec layer, was regenerated exactly, but no PAR law was derived.
   - The typed part couples PAR's derivation to DISCH's sidecar. That is a finding about the pipeline.
2. **The brief, fixed at the prereg, has three defects.**
   - It omits `holon disch --milestone M3`.
   - It orders `holon lossy` before `holon run`, which writes the model lossy reads.
   - It runs replay only "when its help names that milestone", and the help names none.

   None was fixed: the brief is a prereg input.
3. **The reads check contradicts the brief.** The brief requires rewriting `config/holon.json`'s target paths, whose
   pack value is `/root/work/morph`, while the check forbids naming that path in any input. Its absolute-path pattern
   also matches grep regexes after `>`. The mechanical verdict stands. See Question 1.
4. **Parts of M2 and M3 cannot be regenerated from code at one commit.**
   - M2's diagram reads hand-written fact documents.
   - The versioned machines need deployment history from the target's tags and on-chain data.
   - The bundles and the derivation need forge deployment records and corpora tied to M1 models and bindings.

   The session authored none of these: rule 6 forbids hand-written facts, and the rest is not in the code.
5. **The extraction layer is reproducible from code.** 8 documents are byte-identical, the header document and the
   codec layer are equal in content, and the session re-derived the two stripped sinks exactly.
6. **The leak list grew from 327 to 450 matches** between I82's pack (`a5a8624f`) and `<P>`, mostly case_id. The scan
   is pattern-based and reported, never filtered.
