## The rendering deviations, checked

AGENTS rule 2 forbids changing what a law says. Two renderings deviate from a literal transcription of I88's IR, and the coordinator's rule is that each equivalence is checked, not argued.

1. **`blobs_match`, a Bool, rendered as the proposition it decides.**
   - I88's term is a Bool that applies `vh` to every blob ("x's blob hashes (blobhash(i)) are vh of cb(p)'s blobs in order, then zero"). A Bend closure is affine (called at most once), so no Bend def computes that Bool from `vh`.
   - The premise `blobs_match == true` is rendered as `bm_rel(blobhashes(x), vh, cb(p))`. For each index below the longer of the hash list and the blob list, blobhash(i) (zero past the hashes) equals vh of blob i (zero past the blobs). That is exactly the prefix the Bool checks; past it both sides are zero.
   - I88's Bool is stated on `vh`'s image list `gs` of the blobs (`bm_bool`, `blobs_match_b`), and `imgs(vh, cb(p), gs)` ties `gs` to `vh`.
   - `holon.derivation.bendequiv` generates `gen/M3/derivation/bend/equiv/LAWS.bend` and `PROOF.bend`. Bend and BendTT (`--verdict`) both accept its three laws:
     - `blobs_match_of_bool`: `imgs(vh, cb(p), gs)` and `is_true(blobs_match_b(x, gs))` give `blobs_match(x, p, cb, vh)`;
     - `bool_of_blobs_match`: `imgs(vh, cb(p), gs)` and `blobs_match(x, p, cb, vh)` give `is_true(blobs_match_b(x, gs))`;
     - `imgs_unique`: the image list is unique.
   - The proofs are generated: `Bool.cmp`, `Word.cmp` and `U32.is_eq` decide equality (induction on the word's width), then the typed model's `w_eq` over its 8 limbs, then an induction over the two lists.
   - One fact is not in Bend: that an image list exists for every `vh` (it is `List.map`; Bend cannot build it from an arbitrary affine closure). Given that, the rendered premise and I88's are equivalent over the same domain.
   - This rendering replaces the stage-2 draft's `∀ i : Nat` over the zero-extended lists. That one quantified past the prefix the Bool checks, and a `∀` hypothesis is a closure, which the proposition-to-Bool direction would have to use once per index.
2. **`trailing` takes two copies of each closure.**
   - The product def runs the consumer twice (the decoder's count, and the blocks' dequeued sum), and a closure is called at most once.
   - Both copies are the same law binder, rendered from the same IR node: `Render.term` doubles that node's `cb`, `pl` and `dt`.
   - The gate's G2 checks that each copy pair of each `trailing` term in each LAWS.bend is one binder.

## How a counter-law fits BendTT's budget

- **The budget.** BendTT gives each conversion 4e8 steps and rechecks every conversion from scratch. The whole consumer run (55 to 116 machine steps here) in one conversion ran out of fuel.
- **The design.** The proof generator (`holon.derivation.bendproof`) states each step of the consumer's machine as a closed lemma (`es_<run>_<k>`). It joins the steps under a variable fuel (`tg_`, `tt_`, `chain_<run>`, `fu_<run>`), and reaches the run from the law through open lemmas, over variables, where a conversion stops. To make a step a closed lemma, the model generator flattened the consumer's calls into one machine, with frames on an explicit stack (the refinement re-made: 169 of 169).
- **Four kernel behaviours** each needed a generator change. Each was found by running the kernel binary on the translation, because `bend --verdict` prints only "Sorry - this is a mismatch between the TypeScript implementation, and the formalized BendTT kernel. ...".
  1. A lambda argument enters a type annotated (the kernel's `Term.arg`), so a lemma that takes a closure argument never meets its goal by syntax, and the conversion then evaluates the whole run. No lemma takes a closure: each is specialized to the witness's closures, one per tuple.
  2. The translation writes a law's closure variable eta-long. So the law instantiated at the witness holds `y => (q => cb_w(q))(y)`, never the `cb_w` a lemma names, and the kernel converts without eta. Every statement over the law's closures is a wrapper def applied to the witness's closures (unfolded, it holds them exactly as the law does). A bridge rewrites each consumer term from the law's form to the lemmas' form, over variables.
  3. BendTT normalizes a reusable (`+`) argument of a closed def when it binds it.
     - A bridge with reusable parameters received the witness's step in normal form instead of its name. The bridges' parameters are erased.
     - The same rule made the model's per-element heap stores (a `binary.Read` into a slice, an `append` in place) normalize the whole heap once per byte, and one step of O7's second run ran out of fuel. Those natives now rewrite their cell once and put it back once. This is a model generator change, and the refinement was re-made: 169 of 169 agree, only the model's sha256 moves.
  4. A rewrite motive whose head reduces (`is_true(Nat.is_eq(_, la))`) makes the kernel evaluate the term under it, in `neg`'s open context and without sharing. The contradiction is derived through equation motives (`lt`), and only the literals' comparison goes through `is_true`.
- **The fuel as a closed argument.** In bend2, a lemma whose sides reduce into the machine with distinct big fuel thunks overflowed: "Error: the machine stack overflowed (a deep recursion, or a literal too large to expand)". The fuel is passed as a closed argument (`P.fuel0()`), and `run_bc` matches its batch before it reads the fuel, so an open lemma stops at its variable.

## Surprises

1. **BendTT's budget shaped the proof generator** (see "How a counter-law fits BendTT's budget"). The first whole-run counter-law ran out of fuel in BendTT while bend2 accepted it. Fixing it took the machine flattening, closed per-step lemmas joined by generic ones, and four more generator changes for four kernel behaviours: a lambda argument annotated in a type, closure variables written eta-long, reusable arguments normalized on binding, and motives whose head reduces. Q22 says to fix the generator, not the fuel: no fuel was raised anywhere.
2. **bend2's stack overflow, and the fuel as a closed argument.** A lemma whose sides reduce into the machine with distinct big fuel thunks made bend2 fail with "Error: the machine stack overflowed (a deep recursion, or a literal too large to expand)". The fuel (`U32.to_nat(1000000)`) became a closed argument (`P.fuel0()`), and `run_bc` matches its batch before it reads the fuel.
3. **A model generator change after the refinement.** BendTT normalizes a reusable argument when it binds it, so the model's per-element heap stores (a 32-byte `binary.Read` into a slice; `append` in place) normalized the whole heap once per element, and one step of O7's second run ran out of fuel. `w_bytes` and `w_items` now rewrite their cell element by element and put it back once. Consecutive stores into one cell compose in the cell, so the result is the same, and the refinement agrees: 169 of 169, only the model's sha256 moves (`bend-refine --check` exits 0).
4. **The flattened machine.** Stage 1's model had one trampoline per function with nested calls inside a step. For one step to be one closed lemma, calls became frames on an explicit stack of one global machine (`m_step`, `m_run`). The refinement was re-made after it: 169 of 169.
5. **A misdiagnosis on the way.** I first read BendTT's failures as binder levels differing between closures (`λx0 => ..` against `λx1 => ..` in the translation). The kernel uses de Bruijn indices, so those are one term; the cause was the annotation of lambda arguments (`Term.arg`), which reading the kernel's source showed.
6. **Affinity shaped two renderings,** both checked (see "The rendering deviations, checked"): `blobs_match` as the proposition its Bool decides, and `trailing`'s two copies of each closure.
7. **The commit's call answers.** With search3's default hints, the witness commits reverted at the contract's timeliness check: the hints answered the enqueue-time query with a timestamp that made the rollup look delayed. The engine answers the step's external calls by I85's product rules (the sidecar's `calls`), as I85's product machine does.
8. **bend2 orders live calls.** A def may call only defs above it ("expected : a filled definition (an unfilled law is a dead claim: live code cannot use it)"); the cell-rewrite helpers had to precede their callers.
9. **Bend 2 refuses a destructuring let or a tuple pattern on a value bound after a match**, and a match with tuple patterns on two scrutinees. The equivalence proofs take their pairs as parameters and match them in nested matches.
10. **The two stage-1 instrument amendments** (`spec/M3/amendments.jsonl` lines 66 and 67; lines 64 and 65 are I88's): R1 read DERIVATION.json's "laws" as a dict, and R2 forbade the contract's own amendment route. No claim, status or prediction moved.
11. **Stage 1's P3 missed on one clause:** 21 of the 169 cases have no recorded decompressor answer in I85's environment (8 without a blob, 13 whose parent header is refused first, so the consumer never reaches its blob layer), and rule H2 counts each as a disagreement. HypB itself held on all 132 canonical cases.
12. **The first full engine run left O8 under HypC_ignores_trailing UNKNOWN.** bend2 overflowed its stack ("Error: the machine stack overflowed (a deep recursion, or a literal too large to expand)") while comparing the circuit hypothesis premise's wrapper with the law's instance: unfolding the wrapper into HypC_ignores_trailing's telescope sent it into the consumer terms under the telescope's binders. A hypothesis premise is now proved at its own type; both kernels then accept the counter-law, and the second run decided all five laws. Two earlier runs were killed by a container restart and a pause; nothing they wrote was committed, and the committed outputs are the last run's.
13. **The O8 binds law has no inhabitant here** (Q-I89-b), so G11V fails on it as a finding, as the coordinator directed. gate-M3-partial was already red on G11V's in-flight clause (`thresholds.base_gate_M3`).
