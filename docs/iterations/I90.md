# I90 (= I38b): O28 at 65,536 L1 messages, through the count channels of a Go component. 4 of 4 predictions held, counted

I90 states O28 as a law and refutes it at the real size. O28 is the uint16 accumulator of a batch's L1 messages
(whitepaper §11.3, SORT-width; Appendix A F12.7). `morph-l2/common/batch.BatchData.Append` counts them twice:
- it appends each L1 transaction hash's 32 bytes to `l1TxHashes`;
- it adds `uint16(len(l1TxHashes))` to the uint16 field `l1TxNum`.

At 65,536 L1 messages the second count is 0 while the first holds 65,536 hashes. The submitter commits `l1TxNum` as
`numL1Messages`, and DataHashV2 hashes both.

How I90 reaches it:
- **The component:** a Go count component (holon-gocount/0.1) over the carrier `[]common.Hash`, named in the new sidecar
  `spec/M3/lib/go_counts.json`. Its channels are the stores of a pointer-receiver method that record one parameter's
  length:
  - a wrap channel: `l1TxNum`, the length realized in 16 bits;
  - an item channel: `l1TxHashes`, one 32-byte item per element.

  Each channel is one CODEC edge from the carrier to its length count, citing its function.
- **The derivation:** rule PAR's existing branch producer (P3) derives one law over the two channels. The cycle producer
  (P8) records its element as a duplicate. No producer, catalog or frontend changes.
- **The model:** the carrier is a parameter's length, a `Nat` (parametric counts: no per-element cell). A wrap channel
  of width w is `U32.to_nat(U32.and(U32.from_nat(n), 2^w - 1))`, exact for every n.
- **The replay:** a generated internal test of `common/batch` runs the witness on the real `Append`.

The key is `iterations.I90` in `spec/M3/predictions.json`. The contract is the docstring of
`tests_m3/test_go_counts.py`, rules C1-C10. Both were committed in `f3fc92c` (C_K), on `45d01fc`, which adds
BUILD_PLAN rows I90 and I91 to the lead `ebcc2d5`. I38's compression half (HypZ, HypZ_len, O5, O6, D3) is I91 (=
I38c), a later key: I90 is the split that lands soonest.

**O28, found and replayed at the real size:**
- **`L_par_br_batch_BatchData_Append_l1TxHashes_batch_BatchData_Append_l1TxNum` is REFUTED.** Its statement is forall
  x: SliceHash. `batch_BatchData_Append_l1TxHashes(x) == batch_BatchData_Append_l1TxNum(x)`.
  - The witness is x = 65536, the first candidate (2^16), with no reduced N. The difference is 65536 against 0.
  - bend accepts the counter-law (exs x: Nat) in about 1 s, and BendTT's verdict is `checked`, in about 3 s.
  - On the real code, one `Append` on `new(BatchData)` with 65,536 zero hashes leaves 65,536 items in `l1TxHashes`
    and `l1TxNum` 0. The replay is `passed`, provenance library_api.
- **The record's filter bin is `pending`** (concretization `go_only`). The node-level path, the submitter's BatchCache
  packing blocks whose L1 messages sum to 65,536, is I39's.
- **The instrument shows the same wrap across blocks:** two calls of 32,768 and 64 calls of 1,024 (block-sized counts).

**Counted:** all four predictions, with one disclosure the human should weigh (Question 4).

## Surprises

1. **BendTT runs out of fuel on Base's `Nat.divmod` well below 65,536.** A generic kernel probe before the prereg, with
   no law of I90's, measured:
   - bend checks `Nat.divmod(n, n)` for n up to 65,536 in under 0.6 s;
   - BendTT `--verdict` checks it at 1,024 and 4,096;
   - from 16,384 on, BendTT reports "Sorry - this is a mismatch between the TypeScript implementation, and the
     formalized BendTT kernel".

   Run on its own, the kernel binary says "out of fuel": the message is a fuel limit, not a disagreement between the
   kernels. So rule C4 writes a wrap channel as a U32 round trip, which both kernels check at 65,536 (BendTT took about
   3 s on the law's counter-law). The probe is disclosed in the key (`thresholds.model_check_probe`).
2. **A `holon run --laws` subset resets `results/M3/summary.json` and `gen/M3/model/`.** My first draft of rule C9 ran
   the new law alone. That would have dropped the other 84 PAR laws from the summary and deleted the other models.
   The prereg self-check caught it before C_K, and C9 runs every law the summary lists plus the new one, as I67 did.
3. **The predictions schema has no top-level `questions` key** for an iteration (additionalProperties false). The
   questions sit under `thresholds.questions`. This was caught before C_K.
4. **The count pair has no E6 atom, by design.** Two channels of one function share every name token, so I14's lexicon
   would relate them at E6, and an E6-only law is triaged an intent question. The count layer computes no atom (rule
   C2): P3's basis, two paths of one function, is the link, and the record keeps the default triage `finding`.
5. **I82's R1 and R3 fail on this unmerged branch, and pass once it is merged.** `tests_m3/_i82.py::own_commits` walks
   HEAD's first-parent line from I82's prereg and stops at the commit that adds `docs/iterations/I82.md`. On the lead
   that commit is on a merge's second parent, so the walk never stops. Every non-merge commit on the first-parent line

   - `test_r1_own_commits_touch_only_what_the_contract_allows` and
     `test_r3_no_generator_closure_file_changes_and_holon_names_no_law` fail on them.
   - At the lead `f30e0dd` both pass.
   - In a scratch worktree, a `--no-ff` merge of this branch into `f30e0dd` (never pushed) passes both: after the PR's
