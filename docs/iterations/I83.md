## Surprises

1. **A fifth construct, L5: a shift by a non-constant amount.** P1's derivation counted four constructs (L1-L4) in the
   V3-V10 commit actions. The build found a fifth in the same actions: V3's and V4's `_bitmap >> rem` (BatchCodecV0's
   skipped-message bitmap) shifts by a loop-carried amount.
   - L5 compiles EVM SHR/SHL (an amount of 256 or more gives 0) by the constant shifts of the amount's bits below 256.
   - The helper (w_shrv, w_shlv) is emitted only into a model that uses it.
   - The same commit actions also carry a pointer into the memory copy of input bytes (`X + 34 + 60*i`), a loop-carried
     word. L3 compiles it to its offset, as model3 already does for a pointer into a region.
   - P1 held anyway: it predicted no model debt, not the number of constructs.
2. **BendTT ran out of fuel on the V3-V5 commit steps; L6 fixes it in the generator (a kernel finding).**
   - **What happened.** The first full `holon versions inhabit` found candidates at all eight edges. Bend accepted all
     eight inhabitants. BendTT accepted five and failed on V3->V4, V4->V5 and V5->V6 with bend2's "mismatch between the
     TypeScript implementation and the formalized BendTT kernel". The pinned kernel binary, run on bend2's `-o .bendtt`
     translation, says `In r_6: out of fuel` (its FUEL is 4e8, `bendtt.lean` line 157). This is a budget, not a
     rejection.
   - **Bisected** (scratch, never committed), on V5->V6's `ok_6`:
     - **The program, cut statement by statement.** One probe per cut, each a copy of the V5 module with one top-level
       statement replaced by its input. All pass; kernel time:
       - statements 47..39: 4.4 s, the load and setup baseline;
       - 47..29: 8.6 s;
       - 47..28 plus the block loop (Rollup.sol:766-773): 11.4 s;
       - plus the message loop (779-797): 15.3 s;
       - 47..14: 16.9 s.
       Cutting at 10 runs out of fuel at 21.1 s.
     - **One expression at a time**, repeated 3, 10, 30 or 40 times in one lemma:
       - a checked product by a literal, w_mul_ok (64 limb pairs of 32-bit words multiplied bit by bit in the kernel),
         costs about 2.4 s, about 6e7 kernel steps;
       - w_mul_small costs 0.19 s;
       - an L3 copy read costs about 0.01 s;
       - a w_le against a literal costs nothing measurable.
     - **The cause.** The V3-V5 commit step evaluates three checked products by 60 (BatchCodecV0's `numBlocks * 60`, twice,
       and the block loop's `i * 60`) and about 25 products by 58 or 60. Together they put it over 4e8. The V6-V10 steps
       have none.
   - **Fixed in the generator**, as Q22 asks: no fuel, budget or kernel change. L6 is the kernel's evaluation shape of a
     product by a literal, with the values unchanged:
     - the checked product's test is `x <= (2^256 - 1) // c`, one comparison, exact for c >= 1; a literal 0 never
       reaches it (x * 0: no check);
     - the product by c < 2^32 skips zero limbs (w_mulc, w_mul_small's value on every input).
     No model that compiled before has such a product, so R1 holds. After L6, BendTT accepts V3->V4, V4->V5 and V5->V6
     (46.7 s, 47.9 s, 43.9 s).
   - **Shown equal.** TestL6 (new, after C_K, at the coordinator's request) has Bend decide L6's forms and the old ones on
     0, 1, 2^256 - 1, q = (2^256 - 1) // c, q + 1 and fixed-seed random words, for seven literals, each side against
     the integer product.

## Questions for the human

1. **The lifecycle kind `model_extension` (the coordinator's decision, made under your standing delegation; revocable).**
   - **What it does.** A law's binding may change in its cone alone under a fixed statement when four things hold:
     - X3: the new model files are a conservative extension of the bound ones.
     - X4: the law's verdict is re-established on the new model by Bend and BendTT. REFUTED stays REFUTED; PROVED stays
       PROVED or becomes REFUTED; UNKNOWN may become any status.
     - X2: no other binding key moves.
     - X5: G2's tie covers it.
   - **Direction.** It is unsigned and runs in the strengthening direction only: more behaviours, never fewer. It changes
     no claim, statement or registry hash, and a refuted law keeps its witness.
   - **Two variants of X3, both mechanical, both recorded next to the kind.**
     - **Field gain.** A constructor may gain fields: they are erased from every pattern and construction before the
       comparison, and no old clause may read one. The coordinator's conditions:
       - (a) the comparison is byte for byte after erasure, and a negative test shows an old clause reading a gained
         field is rejected;
       - (b) the roots keep their text;
       - (c) X4 applies.
     - **Renaming.** Local names may be renamed. The renaming is inferred deterministically, recorded in the entry,
       re-inferred by the check and verified, never trusted:
       - kind-preserving: definitions, fields and locals only, never a type or constructor;
       - injective and capture-free;
       - it fixes the roots, every name the law's files reference and every cross-file reference;
       - no definition's image is bound as a local in the new file;
       - after it, the comparison is byte for byte.
       Ten tests exercise it: the positive control, the coordinator's eight negatives and kind preservation.
   - **Use.** Ten lines: the PAR-init law (`00630258`, now line 798) and I29's 9 NAT laws (`26620971`, lines
     799-807), all over the versioned model that gained every commit action.
   - Please confirm or revoke the kind and its two variants.
2. **Stable loop numbering.** model3 numbers loops module-wide in compile order, so any change that makes a refused action
   compile renames the loop-local names of every later action. The renaming variant absorbs that.
   - A later key could number loops by (action, ordinal) instead, as one deliberate binding_change over every model.
   - It would change every model's text (the M3 models, V11's module), so it is not for now.
3. **P1's derivation counted four constructs; the build needed six.**
   - L5 (a variable shift) and L6 (the products' evaluation shape) are generic, emitted only into models that use them,
     and leave every earlier model byte-identical.
   - L6 is no language extension: it is the kernel's evaluation shape, as A30's work was. The contract's L1-L4 list did
     not foresee it.
   - Should the contract have admitted A30-style evaluation-shape changes explicitly?
4. **I80's note on model3's callok** (an EOA's empty return data counted as success, model3.py's callok and
   prelude3.py) is not addressed here. I83's commit actions call the labelled stand-in, which returns data, so no
   inhabitant depends on it. It stays a separate follow-up.
5. **Lead-first append-only logs and a branch head's G2.**
   - LANE_RULES put the lead's lines first when a branch merges the lead. `merged_lines` requires the log to extend
     every version along HEAD's first-parent chain, so after such a merge, with lines on both sides, G2 and
     `holon bind` fail on the branch head. They pass on the PR's merge ref, which CI checks out.
   - Should `merged_lines` accept a merge whose second parent's log is the prefix, with the first parent's lines kept
     as side lines? Or should branches order a lead merge's logs first-parent first, and the lead re-order them at the
     PR merge?
6. **The model_extension store** (`laws/STATEMENT_DIFF.store/`) is agent-writable, like the log and the bindings:
