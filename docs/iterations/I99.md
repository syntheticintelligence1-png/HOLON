## G9 in every milestone

`holon.records.premise_gap(law, rec)` is the check, and `premise_problems` the report.
- A record's premises must hold every assume clause of its law, each as its rendering (`holon.ir.premises`, or the law
  set's own renderer).
- Oracle and cryptographic premises may follow the clauses.
- It is a count and a content check in one: a premise list that is short, or that holds another string in a clause's
  place, fails.

It runs in every G9:
- `holon/gate.py`'s g9: M0, M1, M2, and M3's other laws;
- M3's per-family G9: the typed I30 laws, DISCH, FACT, the contract laws, both bundle law sets, and the derivation law
  set (with `render_premise`).

**A failure names every law it holds,** with the clauses each record holds, in one problem placed first. So the
families' cut at six problems never drops a law id. For example: `contract laws: premises do not hold their law's
assume clauses, one readable string per clause (1 records: L_contract_C_o12_m3 (0 of 4))`.

## Surprises

- **The re-made REFUTED bundle records lose their final bin until the final filter runs again.** The decider writes
  its provisional bin ("finding") and no concretization. Only the filter gives a record its final bin, so a re-make of a
  REFUTED law must end with the filter, as the landing template does.
- **I98's final filter is not idempotent on its document.**
  - `results/M3/refute/filter.json` lists under `unbound` only the records that run moves. Run again on the landed
    tree, the filter drops the list, because the 15 records I98 moved are no longer REFUTED.
  - This PR commits the filter's own output. The 15 records keep their bounds and `replay_binding` attempts.
- **Two writers hard-coded `premises: []`,** and five more still do: DISCH (`holon/disch/run.py`), NAT, typed SORT
  (`holon/sorts/run3.py`), SYM-member and the cutover writer. Their laws have no clauses today, so their records are
  complete. If one ever gains a clause, G9 now names it.
