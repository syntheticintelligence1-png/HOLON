## What was built

### (a) The scale wall

**Measured first** (before the prereg, on planted documents and Morph only; the counted profile is
`results/M6/I110/sort_profile.json`):
- Morph's transitions document (1.2 MB) sorts in 0.1 s. Every committed corpus transitions document sorts in under
  0.2 s. Neither the corpus nor Morph has a document that is slow: the corpus's largest Solidity units (c02's CToken
  and CErc20, c10/c15/c20's validator) do not extract at the 4,000,000-node term tier, and at a far larger budget
  CToken's extraction reached 11 GB of memory and was stopped.
- So I planted one: a generated contract whose actions inline `f_k(x) = f_{k-1}(x) + f_{k-1}(x + k)` (depth 6 and
  10, four actions: 1.9 MB and 45 MB documents). cProfile on the 45 MB one: the time is roughly linear in the
  document, and two costs stand out. `holon.sorts.solve.System` was built **twice** over the same constraints
  (`find_conflicts`'s last `satisfiable(active)`, then `System(active)` again in `Model.solve`): 13.4 of 27 s. And
  every affine and compare constraint's text re-rendered its whole subterm (`holon.sorts.extracted.text`, 2.5 M
  calls for 213 k constraints).

**Two speedups, byte-identical by construction and by test (P3):**
- `find_conflicts` returns the System it built for the final active set; `solve` uses it. System is a pure function
  of its constraint list, and its one piece of state (Component's role cache) fills lazily the same way.
- `text(t, memo)`: within one `build()`, a cache keyed by node identity (the cache holds the node, so the id stays
  unique). It returns the same string.

**The budget** (`holon/sorts/budget.py`): a deterministic work meter, never wall-clock. A typed sidecar may name
`sort_budget`. A unit is one step of the pass's own loops: a term node the walk visits, an affine-table row a
propagation sweep reads, a torsor-search node, a constraint a System reads, a site candidate, a member-propagation
step, a union writer scan. The count depends only on the document and the code, so outputs stay byte-identical across
runs and machines (AGENTS rule 7). A document over budget is a recorded miss: `gen/<M>/sorts/budget.json` lists every
document with its work and status, and the miss's model gets every variable unrefined (or no variable, when the walk
itself ran out), with no conflict, site or union. The tier, `typed.sort_budget` 5,000,000, is fixed in the prereg
(about 50 s per document at the ~1e5 units/s measured on the planted 45 MB document). `timeouts_s.sorts` is 7,200
(48 documents all at the tier stay under it). Without the key nothing is metered (`tick()` is a no-op).

No fourth route: an opt-in of `holon eval holon` itself, refused unless the protocol has a `compose` key. Per entry:

2. For every Go package, I105's Go machine frontend, from the same source and config, with

## Soundness

- **The budget only removes information.** A miss's variables get the unrefined sort, the top of the sort lattice:
  the same answer the pass gives a variable no constraint reaches. No conflict, site or union of it is written, so
  nothing downstream can use a refinement the pass did not compute. A sort refines the type a model gives a value
  and adds no assumption. So a law over an opaque document is checked under weaker premises (it may become UNKNOWN
  or fail types), never under stronger ones. Nothing over a miss can be PROVED that the full pass would not also
  prove. The miss is never silent: `budget.json`, the sorts stage's detail and the row's `sort_budget` record it.
- **The speedups change no byte** (P3): reusing a pure function's result, and caching a pure render.
- **Q62 carried to diagram and evidence**: an empty component set derives no codec, PAR-diagram or evidence law. It
  only adds UNKNOWN-or-absent, never PROVED. The refusal stays wherever a link sidecar exists.
- **Composed driver**: inferred roles are installed only where no sidecar exists, so they can never replace a hand
  sidecar. Every law they feed is decided by the checker as before. REFUTED still needs its replay: the replay stage
  is route A's (`not_applicable`, no binding), and so is the filter's classification (unreplayed). Per-law verdicts
  are withheld (Q56).
- `holon/` names no target. Every change is justified by the pass's own structure, and the planted document, never
  a corpus entry.

## Surprises

1. **The scale wall was one document per target**, not the whole system. A 1.6 MB EigenLayer document puts the
   torsor-role search (`holon.sorts.solve._search`) into exponential backtracking. Every other sealed document sorts
   in well under a second per MB. The budget is what fixes it, not the speedups (about 25%).
2. **The corpus and Morph have no slow document.** The corpus's largest Solidity units do not extract at the 4M-node
   tier. The Go machine documents are large (up to 790 MB) but linear: 947,501 units in 6.8 s. So the speedup had to
   be found on a planted document, as the brief allows.
3. **The composed route's next wall is `holon types`.** It refuses a whole milestone over one ill-sorted term:
   - whole-map writes (I109's A4, still refused) on 5 Go entries;
   - a record used as a word on 3 Go entries and on EigenLayer;
   - nil-map debt on c19;
   - Solidity's "length of a w value" on c05;
   - "an update at a path deeper than one member" on Aave.

   This is I107's sort-pass refusal pattern, one stage later.
4. **Route C's stage order mattered.** derive checks that `gen/<M>/evidence.json` is what `holon evidence` writes on
   the final tree, so evidence must run after sorts .. disch. Also, `holon diagram` and `holon evidence` refused a
   milestone with no component at all, even under Q62. Both were found in the rehearsal (not counted).
5. **The go-machines runner under I110's protocol** stops at derive on c12 and c16 (I109: complete). The protocol's
   milestone is M9, whose derive needs the adoption sidecar the composed route installs. That runner never installs
   roles. P1 is about its output's `machines.nil_maps` only.
6. **c12 lost its extraction in the counted run** (P5), very likely to my `go clean -cache` during the run.
7. **Withholding destroyed the plaintext.** Under `--roles`, `withhold()` deleted the per-law outputs after hashing
   them, so a sealed REFUTED's plaintext would have been lost. `--vault` keeps it outside the repository.
