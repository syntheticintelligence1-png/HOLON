## Done

- **`holon/gen/eqlib.py`, the Base lemma library** (46 definitions, 30,417 bytes). It covers U32 as Base's Word of
  32 Bools, and Bool/Cmp identities:
  - `is_eq` against `xor` and `or` (`lbu_eqz_xor`, `lbu_eqz_or`), `is_eq`/`is_lt` reflexivity and small constants;
  - addition and subtraction **only as zero/one identities** (`lbu_add_z`, `lbu_z_add`, `lbu_sub_z`, `lbu_add_one`,
    `lbn_inc`).

  **Correction (review):** an earlier version of this record said the library also holds addition with carry,
  subtraction with borrow, and a byte assembled by shifts and masks against bit by bit. It does not (no `shl`, `shr`,
  `U32.and` or `Word.and` occurs in it). The general carry/borrow facts are rediscovered per helper by lemma
  discovery (for example `q_w_add__c226 {U32.add(p, b2u(c)) == ite(c, inc(p), p)}`, again as
  `q_w_add_ok__c52840`, and `q_w_sub__c189`, `q_w_sub__c350`), and `u_b4`/`u_be4` are proved by opening the U32 into
  bits, not by a library lemma. This deviates from Q31 item 1 (see Questions for the human).

  Each lemma is stated for every input of its types. It is proved by induction over `Word(n)`, whose step is a
  finite lemma checked by exhaustive case analysis, or by honest finite case analysis. The text is fixed: every name
  starts with `lb`, and no model, target or definition is named (tested). It checks alone in bend2 and BendTT.
- **`holon/gen/eqprove.py` and `holon/gen/eqterms.py`, the proof classes.** They hold:
  - case split with reflection (`U32.is_eq(a, b) == True` → `a == b`, then the same for `w_eq`);
  - lemma discovery driven by the difference of the two sides (the differing subterms generalized into a lemma for
    all values of its parameters), proved by the same engine, by induction when a side recurses. **Correction
    (review):** an earlier version said "accumulator specialization (one lemma per constructor ... by mutual
    induction)". That mechanism is not implemented: eqprove enumerates no constructors of an accumulator and does no
    mutual induction (BEND_PRIMER §4 forbids mutual recursion). `kw_set`'s proof uses one discovered lemma,
    `q_kw_set__c78 {p == P.kw_put(p, k, v, True{})}`, plus the derived reflection `q_kw_set__r_w_eq`. The label is now
    "lemma discovery with induction";
  - bit-by-bit evaluation, and use of the Base rules.

  They are wired into `holon.gen.equivalence` as further classes. The F31 criterion is unchanged: every root is
  extensionally equal on every input, and family roots are inter-derivable both ways.
- **`holon/sorts/prelude3_lemmas.py`, the list lemmas `h_get`'s rewrite needs.** Since the review, the statements of
  `lp_rev_rev`, `lp_beq_refl`, `lp_filt_nil` and `lp_filt_con` are fixed and their proofs are generated at use time by
  `eqprove` (`text()`, deterministic); `lp_beq_sound`, `lp_rev2`, `lp_beq_rev_h` and `lp_beq_rev` stay hand-written
  (question below). The module is now imported statically, so it is in the generator closure. They are stated for all
  lists:
  - `lp_rev_rev` and `lp_rev2` (reversal);
  - `lp_beq_refl`, `lp_beq_sound` and `lp_beq_rev` (`b_eq`);
  - `lp_filt_nil` and `lp_filt_con` (a filter over cons).

  They are used only for a model whose prelude definitions are the prelude's own text (`applies`). They check in
  both kernels.

**Cost diagnosis (corrected; the reviewer's instrumented measurement, not mine).** A round does not re-check the whole
growing LEMMAS.bend once: `_lemma_file` includes only the proven lemmas the frontier uses. A round makes N+1 full
bend2 checks of that file, where N is the number of candidate classes that fail in the round: after each failed
candidate the file is checked again. On :363, round 0 made 27 bend2 runs (26 failed candidates), 107 s of bend in
118 s wall; round 1 made 37 runs (36 failed candidates), 165 s of 167 s. 62 of the 64 checks were genuine kernel
rejections of a class ("Error: expected/observed"), none an unresolved reference; each took 2.5 to 4 s on a 1.25 MB
file. So the cost is serialized whole-file re-checks after failed candidates, and "0 failed" in the progress lines
counts only definitions for which every class failed. An earlier sentence here, "No class failed on any definition it
reached", was wrong and is withdrawn.

**Re-estimate.** At the observed 1 to 12 minutes per round and at least 124 to 126 rounds per law, one law is many
hours (roughly 2 to 25 hours, with the later, larger rounds toward the top of the range), and the three laws share
nothing today, so a full rebind is of the order of a day or more of machine time, not "an unattended run of hours".

## Surprises

- The real pair module is about 25 times the prelude pair. Proving the prelude pair takes 88 s; the cone of the
  first law (:363) was 42% done after 84 min. The cost is serialized whole-file bend2 re-checks after each failed
  candidate class (corrected diagnosis above), not any one lemma.
- The same discovered lemma is proved again and again: the prelude-pair PROOF.bend holds 24 lemmas `q_w_eq__cNNN`
  with the identical statement (associativity of `Bool.and`), and `q_w_add__c226` / `q_w_add_ok__c52840` state the same
  fact. Discovered lemmas are not shared across definitions (reviewer's nit; left for the redesign).
- `tests_m2/test_target_agnostic_exhaustive.test_generic_code_holds_no_morph_identifier` failed on
  `holon/statement_diff.py` (`bc`, the `bound_commit` local of F31's `_binding_change`), at base `4a86117` too.
  Neither F31 nor the first F32 record reported it. The review fix renames the local to `commit_b`.
- `holon/gen/equivalence.py` and its new imports are in the M1/M2 generator closure. Any change to the equivalence
  generator invalidates every M1 and M2 record's `generator_sha256`, so the records must be regenerated.
- The first frozen-suite run under heavy machine load reported one failure. A verbose rerun passed. A timing-sensitive
  test is the likely cause, but the failing name was not captured.
