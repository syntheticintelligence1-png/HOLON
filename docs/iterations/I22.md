Plan: `docs/BUILD_PLAN.md` §4 I22. Prereg: `f8fa447`, key I22. It contains the instrument `spec/M2/lib/mutation_preregistration.json`, the diff sheet `spec/M2/lib/mutation_diff_sheet.md`, `prompts/I22_mutation_adequacy.md` and `tests_m2/test_mutation_adequacy.py`.

**Status.** No mutant was run and no outcome exists. The catalog was not signed off, so under the plan nothing runs under it. A replacement catalog is preregistered under a new key, I60, once I20 has merged. The I22 predictions are never scored.

## Done

- **Who signed off.** The human delegated both the catalog sign-off and the equivalence verdicts on 2026-10-13 (`docs/HUMAN_ANSWERS.md`). The lead does not sign off a catalog it drafted, so both decisions went to fresh agents with no stake and no access to any prediction.
- **Catalog review: rejected.** Two independent reviewers looked at it, one with a fairness lens and one with a soundness lens. Both declined to sign off. Their full outputs are in `docs/iterations/I22_catalog_review.json`.
  - Both recomputed all 110 mutants from the pinned checkout. Offsets, original text, `mutant_sha256`, `mutants_sha256` and the diff sheet hash all reproduce.
  - The site and rewrite rules are therefore reproducible. The problems lie in what the catalog counts.
- **Blind equivalence panel**, on the rejected catalog. The record is `docs/iterations/I22_equivalence_panel.json`.
  - **Set-up.** Six judges, two per file group, plus refuters on every claim of equivalence. They saw only a trimmed copy of the sheet (the definition, the operators and the 110 diffs) and the pinned source.
  - **Blindness audit.** Every tool call was audited for reads outside the blind folder. There were none.
  - **Result.** 2 equivalent (M057, M058) and 108 not equivalent. Both judges called M018 equivalent, but the refuter overturned it.
  - **Why it is not committed as `spec/M2/equivalent_mutants.json`.** That file requires a signed-off catalog.

## Blocking findings, from the reviewers

1. **The in-scope rule selects on a variable tied to the outcome.** (fairness)
   - **The rule.** Rollup.sol is limited to functions reached from the 8 actions the M1 abstraction models. Go is limited to "code a law reads".
   - **What it leaves out.**
     - 11 matching Solidity sites in the extracted, coverage-certified actions: claimReward's `delete` at :675; cmp sites at :211, :501, :510, :519, :554 and :815; cursor reads at :192, :193, :212 and :686.
     - Extracted Go codecs: `node/types/batch_header.go`, the genesis header encoder in `ops/l2-genesis/.../genesis/batch.go`, and `common/batch/batch_data.go:108`.
   - **Why it matters.** A site no law reads cannot be killed, so leaving such sites out biases the rate upward.
2. **K2 counts proof losses that are not localized obstructions.** (soundness)
   - **The flaw.** "PROVED to UNKNOWN on a changed set input" is attributed at the level of the law set, not the law.
   - **Routes to a false kill.** In-run Bend timeouts and `Unsupported` end as UNKNOWN with exit 0 (`holon/pipeline.py`, `holon/bend.py`). Undefined bytes can also block both proof and refutation, as in M018's unwritten header byte. Any of these lets a law that is still true score as a kill.
   - **Why it matters.** This biases the rate upward and contradicts the instrument's own error clause.
3. **The error clause is too broad.** (soundness)
   - **The flaw.** A failure in any law set, or in any replay (unpredicted laws included), turns the whole mutant into an error. That counts as "not killed", even when a predicted law already satisfied K1.
   - **Why it matters.** This biases the rate downward, and unevenly: Solidity mutants go through three law sets, Go mutants through one.

## Advisory findings that the replacement must address

- **The outcome is largely fixed by construction.** At most 72 mutants can move a predicted law. The other 38 cannot, because:
  - the rollup laws (M1x) are decided over `m1_view`, the hand abstraction minus the guard-missing requires, so no delete, cursor or comparison mutant reaches them;
  - `L_dom_hdr` is REFUTED at baseline, so the length-check mutants are masked;
  - the `dataHash` preimage stores in `_commitBatch` lie outside the projection.
  
  The rejected catalog would mostly have measured its own design. Its forecast of 72 kills was that ceiling.
- **The predicted-law sets are not derived mechanically.** Some sets include laws that cannot read the mutated code, and some miss laws that do. `cmp_boundary` uses "any" law while the other seven operators use fixed sets.
- **The operator set has gaps.**
  - The decoders are never mutated: neither Solidity's `get*` nor the Go getters' slice bounds.
  - `<` and `<=` are not mirrored.
  - M046 and M047 are duplicates.
  - Byte-layout operators dominate the composition.
  
  Per-operator and per-law-family rates are needed.
- **Laws derived only on the mutant can never kill.** That deflates the derived-rule channel, which is the one channel that reacts to new code.
- **Some semantics are wrong or missing.**
  - The panic and error replay semantics conflict with the meanings of `ok_go`, `ok_sol` and `dec_go`.
  - A witness that replays on both versions is a baseline finding the model missed (§9.6), not an extraction defect.
  - No replay harness exists for the laws that can kill.
  - Obstructions behind a baseline-REFUTED law go unmeasured.

## Surprises

- **The drafter's adversarial review missed all three blocking problems.** It even tightened K1, but not K2 and not the scope. The independent catalog review, which the plan reserves for the human, was the step that caught them.
- **Equivalence turned out to be rare and narrow.** Only two mutants are equivalent. Both flip a comparison against 0 on an unsigned value in a loop condition that is always true.
