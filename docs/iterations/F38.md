# F38: G2 verifies claimed signatures, each gate re-checks its own milestone's proofs, the checker's stack

Fix iteration (not preregistered), by the lead in session, on the lead head df99363 (branch `f38`). The human left three
questions from F35's merge to the lead ("whatever as you see best"); the lead's rulings are docs/HUMAN_ANSWERS.md Q39.

## Done

### 1. G2 verifies a claimed signature itself (`holon/statement_diff.py`)

Before: a committed weakening or binding_change line whose acceptance record said `signature: "verified against
keys/"` was taken at its word. A weakening needs nothing more, and a binding_change skipped its equivalence re-check.
Nothing re-verified the signature, so an agent could commit such a claim and bypass AGENTS rule 2 (never weaken a claim)
and F31's equivalence check. No committed line carries or claims a signature (all 263 checked), and keys/ holds no key.

Now `_verify_committed` verifies every signature a line carries or its acceptance record claims, with
`verify_signature_at`: gpg against keys/ as the commit that appended the line had it (a key added later signs nothing
earlier), imported into a throwaway GNUPGHOME as before. A failed verification fails G2 (with no key, every claim
fails: a signature is a human act, Q3). A binding_change skips its equivalence re-check only for a signature that
verifies and that its acceptance record names.

### 2. Each gate re-checks only its own milestone's binding_change proofs (`statement_diff.scan`, `bindings`, CI)

Before (F35): on a git checkout every milestone's G2 re-checked every binding_change line of the log in both kernels,
so gate-M0, gate-M1 and gate-M2 each re-ran the three 7.9 MB M3 proofs (gate-M0: about 28 min on a runner, 2 min
before), and `holon bind`'s check of the change log ran them again.

Now, with the gated milestone named, a committed binding_change line of a law that is not in `laws/<milestone>/` gets
every check of a committed line (acceptance record, kind, facts at the appending commit, a claimed signature) except the
re-check of its proofs, which its own milestone's G2 makes. Such lines are listed in scan's `elsewhere` and named in
G2's note. `bindings._accepted_entries` (behind `check_change_log`, G2's change-log check, and `holon bind`) scans as the
milestone's G2 does, so a gate no longer re-checks other milestones' proofs through that path either. Off git, F35's
rule stands (another milestone's line is not checked at all).

CI: the partial M3 gate now runs G2 (`.github/workflows/holon-ci.yml`), so M3's binding_change proofs are re-checked
once per run, in M3's own gate. M0_SPEC's G2 is about laws/M0; no gate claims another milestone's proofs.

### 3. The checker's JavaScript stack (`holon/bend.py`, `config/holon.json` bend.js_stack_mb)

F33-F35's "the machine stack overflowed" crashes (no verdict; retried under Q34) come from bun's JavaScriptCore stack
cap. Measured with a plain recursion under the pinned bun:

| RLIMIT_STACK | BUN_JSC_maxPerThreadStackUsage | frames before overflow |
|---|---|---|
| 8 MB, 64 MB or 1 GB | default | about 39,900 (the cap; why F35's `ulimit -s` did nothing) |
| 8 MB | 64 MB or 1 GB | about 64,300 |
| 64 MB | 64 MB or 1 GB | about 523,000 |
| 1 GB | 1 GB | about 8,387,000 |

Every checker run now gets the registered stack: `_env()` drops every inherited `BUN_JSC_*` option (as it drops
`BUN_OPTIONS` and `BUN_CONFIG_*`) and sets `BUN_JSC_maxPerThreadStackUsage` to `bend.js_stack_mb` (1024), and `run` and
`run_budgeted` raise the process's RLIMIT_STACK to the same (within the hard limit) before starting the checker, which
inherits it. A stack limit changes no verdict: it only lets a deep or large file get one.

Evidence:
- a 20000-deep expression: "the machine stack overflowed" at bun's default (4 MB, reproduced by the test), a verdict at
  the registered stack (tests_m3/test_f38.py);
- the 7.9 MB whole-cone proof of L_sort_realize_rollup_363_4a64383b (binding_change line 261): bend2 five times at load 3
  to 4, all ACCEPTED, no crash (230.7 to 233.5 s); BendTT (--verdict) once, ACCEPTED (469.4 s). Without it, the F35
  review saw 2 of 3 bend2 runs overflow at load 2 to 4, and F35 about 7 in 10 at load 6 to 10.

Q34's crash retries stay as a backstop (a crash is still never a verdict).

### 4. Tests: `tests_m3/test_f38.py` (new, 6 tests)
