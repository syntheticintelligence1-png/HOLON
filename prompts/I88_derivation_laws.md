## What I88 does

1. **The statements (S1-S4).** Five laws over the product step:
   - binders: a reachable Rollup state `s`, the commit action `x`, the payload `p`, the decoded transactions `ts`, and the oracles `cb`, `pl`, `vh`, `et` and `dt`;
   - the O8 pair adds the circuit `circ`; O24 adds two calldata block-context arguments `c1` and `c2`;
   - the product terms are the sidecar's vocabulary, and their target names (functions, types, fields) live in the sidecar only.

   | law | rule, sub-form | obligation |
   |---|---|---|
   | `L_prod_cycle_lastBlockNumber` | PAR, product_cycle | O7 (F11b,c): the derived last block equals the committed header's `LastBlockNumber`, which the next derivation reads as its parent's |
   | `L_prod_count_l1MsgNum` | PAR, product_count | F11's L1 count (no O-id): the derived sum of `l1MsgNum` equals the committed `L1MessagePopped` |
   | `L_prod_trailing_HypC_binds_trailing` | PAR, product_trailing | O8 (F11a) under HypC_binds_trailing: an accepted batch has no undequeued transaction |
   | `L_prod_trailing_HypC_ignores_trailing` | PAR, product_trailing | the same statement under HypC_ignores_trailing |
   | `L_prod_calldata_BlockContexts` | FACT, product_calldata | O24 (F11f): two version-0 calldata block-context arguments, bound by no commitment, derive the same blocks |

2. **The hypotheses (S3):**
   - HypB: the blob layer recovers what the blob construction encodes;
   - HypT: the transaction codec, M2's own hypothesis of that name;
   - HypC_binds_trailing and HypC_ignores_trailing, of family HypC.

   Each is a premise of every law that uses it, and each has an evaluator instance (the real library, or the canonical circuit).
3. **The law files (L1-L5).**
   - The laws and their registry go to `laws/M3/derivation/`. The 0.3 schema gains an additive product term.
   - The set is its own directory because G2 binds every law at `laws/M3`'s top level through its Bend model (`holon.bindings`, a closure module), and these have none yet.
   - Statement hashes are `holon.gen.refs.law_hash`'s. Inline refs carry the content hashes of the documents the product terms and hypotheses depend on.
4. **The search (W1-W4).** The steps are every commit of I85's product traces. The candidates are a small payload grammar per step:
   - n contexts, numbered from the parent's last block;
   - the mutations `last_number`, `l1_count` and `trailing`;
   - a calldata pair.

   Commits run through I85's `Product.commit` and the consumer through I85's interpreter, with the real library as oracle and the answers recorded. Committed getters run on I24's compiled header component (a Bend model). A law's witness is the first candidate where its premises hold and its claim fails.
5. **The replay (RP1-RP2).** Each witness runs on the real `ParseBatch` (I85's generated Go test, offline).
6. **The records (D1-D4).** Every record is UNKNOWN. Bend's attempt is not run (no consumer model); the evaluator's attempt holds the witness, its observation and its replay. `holon run --milestone M3 --laws <these ids>` routes to `holon derivation decide`.
7. **The split (SP1).** `results/M3/derivation/splits.json`.

## Risks for the build

1. **The model or the oracles on new payloads.**
   - An unsupported interpreter step, or a compressor or decompressor surprise on a 60- or 120-byte payload, makes a candidate unsupported.
   - A predicted witness may then move to a later candidate or vanish. P3 misses, and the record says why.
2. **The Go side runs locally only.** No CI job has the module cache. `decide --check` re-evaluates from recorded answers, with no Go.
3. **The header component's model is compiled by the local Bend for evaluation.** Locally the bun is not the pinned build. The kernel is not involved: nothing is checked, only evaluated.

## Questions for the human (stated at the prereg)

- **Q-I88-a (the rule gap).** Product obligations are plan-stated, not discovered. Should a later catalog add product sub-forms? Candidates:
  - PAR-cycle over TRANS and CONS cycles, closed by a commitment's getter;
  - PAR-consumer over an external consumer's read and write sets;
  - FACT over a consumer's uncommitted reads.
- **Q-I88-b (F11's L1 count has no O-id).** §11.3 maps F11a to O8, F11b,c to O7, F11d to O23 and F11f to O24, but nothing to "L1 counts are discarded". Should it get its own O-id, or fold into O7 as the L1-message cursor's cycle?
- **Q-I88-c (HypC's reading).** The pair follows BUILD_PLAN's direction: binds PROVED, ignores REFUTED. "Binds" is read as "the circuit accepts no batch whose payload carries undequeued transactions". Under the other reading, "the circuit executes trailing transactions", a consumer-agreement law would fail under binds instead. Is my reading the intended one?
- **Q-I88-d (O24 at the head).** The head's join never sets calldata contexts; only the legacy ABIs do. Derivation decodes the calldata of the transaction that emitted the commit event, which need not be a direct call of the Rollup. Should a later key join the legacy ABIs and that route, so that O24's exploitability at the head is decided rather than conditional?
- **Q-I88-e (the law set's place).** Should I89 move these laws to `laws/M3`'s top level once it binds them?
- **Q-I88-f (the M3 comparison).** I82's `holon report` reads `results/M3`'s top level only, so O7, O8 and O24 still show no law in `docs/M3_COMPARISON.md`. Should it read this law set?
