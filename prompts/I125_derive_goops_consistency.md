## The gap (F-M6-05; Q105 (6); Q117 (3))

PAR derives a totality law over every op of a Go-op document. holon.gen.goloop writes an op outside its classes as the
line `# <op>: unsupported (<reason>)` and defines neither `<op>` nor `ok_<op>`. A law over such an op names something
its model never defines, so `holon run --unbound unknown` records it UNKNOWN by its binding error, with no checker run.
UNKNOWN is the right outcome (no instrument defect); but the record says "cannot be bound", not why. At corpus scale this
is 234 of I124's baseline's 268 derived laws (188 on `ok_<op>`, 46 on the op's name), every one a PAR law on a Go-op
component. Derive is the side out of step under the opaque reading; goops is right to refuse.

## Build: choice (B), emission with the reason

1. **holon run --unbound-reason goops** (with `--unbound unknown`), set by the protocol opt-in
   `run_unbound_reason "goops"` (spec/M6/lib/i125_run.json; holon.eval.holon_run.runner_args passes it to the
   milestone runner only). In holon.codecrun, for a Go-op law (kind `goops`) whose binding fails (the pre-check or the
   check after the decision), if its statement text (comments stripped) names as `M.<op>` or `M.ok_<op>` an op that the
   generated model file writes as `# <op>: unsupported (<reason>)`, the record is UNKNOWN with one attempt
   `{kind: op_refused, ops: [{op, class, reason}], binding_error}` and a bound naming goops' refusal. `class` is
   `straight-line` where the reason is the straight-line class's (I117), `loop` otherwise (goloop's loop classes, I67
   and I122). The reason is read from the generated file, never written by hand.
2. **Nothing else moves.** Derive takes no new input: the same laws are derived with and without the opt-in. A law that
   binds is decided exactly as before; a law whose binding fails for any other reason keeps its binding attempt; no
   verdict changes; nothing is special-cased by law id or target; holon/ names no target.
3. **Aggregates.** The composed eval's run stage adds, per runner and only under the opt-in, `op_refused_unknown` and
   `op_refused_classes` (counts by class) beside `binding_unknown`.

Why (B) and not (A): (A) shrinks the derived set, which is close to AGENTS.md rule 2, and the lead prefers (B). (B) keeps
every denominator: the derived, decided (PROVED and REFUTED together, Q56) and UNKNOWN counts are the same with and without
the opt-in, so nothing can read as a gain.

## Soundness

The opt-in only rewrites the reason of a record that is already UNKNOWN with no decision (the binding failed, so no law or
proof file, no witness, no checker verdict). It never touches a law that binds. It cannot make any law PROVED or REFUTED,
and every REFUTED still needs its replay exactly as before.
